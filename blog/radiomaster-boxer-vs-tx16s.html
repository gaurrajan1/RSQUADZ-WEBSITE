Radiomaster Boxer — The Compact Powerhouse

The Radiomaster Boxer was designed around one idea: give FPV pilots everything they need and nothing they don't. Its game-style form factor sits naturally in your hands, making it the go-to choice for Indian pilots who fly freestyle or race micro and 5-inch quads. At roughly ₹12,500–₹16,000 through RSQUADZ's Radiomaster catalogue, it's also the more wallet-friendly option — a real consideration when you're budgeting for an entire build in India, where customs and shipping can add up.

Under the hood, the Boxer packs AG01 CNC aluminium hall-effect gimbals — the same units found in Radiomaster's premium controllers. You get smooth, precise stick movement with virtually no deadzone, which matters when you're threading gaps at speed. It runs EdgeTX firmware out of the box, giving you full model configuration, mixer programming, and Lua script support. The 1.3-inch OLED display is minimal, but it's clear and does the job for in-field adjustments. If you've flown with a colour screen before, you'll barely miss it — most pilots configure models at home and rarely touch the screen at the field.

Where the Boxer truly earns its reputation in India is portability. If you travel between flying spots — say, weekend trips outside Bangalore or Mumbai — the compact size fits in a backpack alongside your goggles and quad. Pick the ELRS version for the best range-per-rupee ratio, and you'll have a long-range link that outperforms systems costing twice as much. For beginners looking to pair with a Walksnail or DJI O3 VTX, the Boxer handles the control side cleanly and affordably.

Radiomaster TX16S Mark II — The Full-Featured Flagship

The TX16S Mark II is Radiomaster's flagship, and it shows. The 4.3-inch colour IPS touchscreen is the headline feature — it turns model setup into a visual experience rather than a scrolling-through-menus one. EdgeTX's full interface comes alive on this display: you can see your channel outputs, telemetry graphs, and mixer pages without squinting. For Indian pilots who build and tune multiple models — freestyle quads, long-range wings, cinematography rigs — the TX16S makes juggling those setups genuinely easier.

The TX16S Mark II shines in protocol flexibility. The 4-in-1 multi-protocol module supports FrSky, Flysky, DSMX, and dozens of other protocols, which means you can bind to nearly anything without swapping hardware. If you're also adding an external ELRS module in the JR bay, you get the best of both worlds — legacy protocol support for older models and cutting-edge ELRS for new builds. This versatility is particularly useful in India's FPV community, where pilots often accumulate receivers from different eras and brands.

Build quality on the Mark II is solid. The CNC-machined AG01 gimbals are identical to the Boxer's, so stick feel is just as precise. The radio's larger frame gives it a more traditional transmitter feel — if you've used a Taranis X9D or a Jumper T16, the hand position will feel immediately familiar. The six toggle switches and two slider potentiometers give you physical controls for arming, flight modes, camera tilt, and anything else you want to map. Expect to pay around ₹18,000–₹24,000 in India through RSQUADZ, depending on the module configuration you choose.

Which One Should You Buy in India?

The honest answer depends on three things: your budget, how many different types of models you fly, and how much you value portability.

Choose the Boxer if: you're focused on FPV quads (freestyle, racing, or cinewhoops), you want to keep costs down while still getting premium gimbals and EdgeTX, or you travel to flying spots and need something that packs light. The Boxer ELRS version is arguably the best value Radiomaster transmitter you can buy in India right now. It's also an excellent first "real" controller for Indian beginners stepping up from toy-grade drones — check our guide to importing drone components in India if you're putting together your first complete FPV setup.

Choose the TX16S Mark II if: you fly multiple protocol types (FrSky, Flysky, DSMX alongside ELRS), you want a touchscreen for faster model configuration, or you're building professional/commercial platforms where the extra switches and screen real estate help manage complex setups. If you're a club member in Pune, Hyderabad, or Delhi NCR who helps newcomers bind their gear, the TX16S's multi-protocol support makes you the person everyone hands their receiver to.

Neither choice is wrong. Both transmitters share the same gimbal quality, the same firmware ecosystem, and both are well-supported with replacement parts. The Boxer is the sharper tool; the TX16S is the deeper toolbox.
